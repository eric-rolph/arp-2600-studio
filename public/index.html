FIELD GUIDE / 01

A conversation with the circuit.

1 / Make a sound

Power on. Click or touch the four-octave keyboard, or use A W S E D F T G Y H U J K. Z and X shift octaves. The synth starts monophonic with last-note priority. DUO sends the lower note to VCO 1/3 and patches the upper note to VCO 2; envelopes remain shared. Hold Gate to trigger manually. MIDI adds pitch bend, mod wheel and sustain pedal.

2 / Give it a voice

Use headphones. Select Droid · voice + circuit, then enable Microphone. Speak and play together: your voice enters the ring modulator, and its envelope opens the amplifier and filter. The green trace shows voice; orange shows the result. In this patch, a quiet microphone means a quiet synth.

3 / Break the normal

Every outlined output jack can drive any input jack. Click an output, then an input; click a connected input to remove its cable. Unpatched inputs return to their printed internal sources. “Show signal path” opens an accessible routing list. Faders respond to mouse, touch and arrow keys; double-click resets a fader.

4 / Perform to tape

Record captures the instrument and a separate dry microphone stem. Stop preserves the take. Raise DRY to blend the original voice back in. Varispeed links pitch and time; reverse, wow, flutter and saturation color playback. Each take has speed, start offset and level controls. Play takes while recording to layer another performance.

Export mix renders the audible take settings into a stereo 16-bit WAV. Stem downloads preserve the original recording. Takes are held in memory: export before closing or reloading. Patches can be saved in this browser or exported as JSON.

5 / Recover

Panic releases notes and resets the synthesizer feedback and reverb. Microphone toggles the physical input off. If a browser denies input access, change the site permission and try again. Chrome or Edge is recommended for MIDI.

Model fidelity & sources

This is an ARP 2600-inspired digital instrument, not an official ARP/Korg product or a verified circuit-exact emulation. It models normalized patching, three oscillators, ring multiplication, envelope following, ADSR/AR, noise color, sample-and-hold, a voltage processor, a clocked switch, and a nonlinear four-pole filter.

Oscillators use PolyBLEP edges at 2× sample rate. The ladder filter and spring-like reverb are approximations; component tolerances, exact 4012/4072 responses, physical spring dispersion, tape hysteresis and mechanical tape behavior have not been measured against hardware. Arbitrary feedback paths have causal sample delay. Duophonic mode provides upper/lower pitch with shared envelopes; there are no independent polyphonic voices. Voltage ranges and control layout are adapted for a screen. Tape uses resampling, saturation and speed modulation rather than a magnetic-domain simulation.
